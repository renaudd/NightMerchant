*Geta Dying in his Mother’s Arms*, Jacques-Augustin-Catherine Pajou, 1766–1828 (Staatsgalerie Stuttgart)

The year is 211 A.D. You are a merchant peddling staples and extravagances in the city of Rome during the sunset of its Golden Age.  
   
Rome wants Grain to feed its people, Meat Animals for its banquets, Exotic Animals for its games, Weapons for its troops, Marble for public works, and Wine for, of course, everyone.  
   
This is wonderful news! But there’s a catch.

Back in 45 B.C., Julius Caesar enacted the *Lex Iulia Municipalis* to reduce traffic and congestion in the city: heavy commercial wagon deliveries must take place at night.  
   
Therein lies the challenge.

Rome is not arranged on a grid. At this time it was an overcrowded maze of meandering paths, split by the bends of the Tiber River. And there was no street lighting, so losing one’s way was a common occurrence.  
   
To make matters worse, night is when Rome’s seedy underbelly emerges. Nefarious bandits, corrupt legions, plastered patricians; any encounter is likely to be a bad one.  
   
And to cap things off, in this particular year, Rome is embroiled in serious drama.  
   
The respected authoritarian, emperor Septimius Severus, died at the beginning of the year while on campaign in Britain. He left the empire in the hands of his two sons, **Bassianus** (whom history remembers by his nickname, **Caracalla**, and his official imperial title, *Marcus Aurelius Antoninus*) and **Geta**, now in early adulthood. His dying instruction was for them to “be harmonious, enrich the soldiers, and scorn all other men.”  
   
Neither of these young co-emperors were big on the stoics of rule. They spent their time feasting, drinking, and gambling at the races and gladiatorial games.  
   
And the emperors did not heed their father’s directive to be harmonious. They fought constantly, immediately setting out to split the empire in two so the boys would not have to work together.  
   
The year 211 would be the only year that the two brothers would spend sharing power. On December 26th, Bassianus (Caracalla) called for a parley with Geta at the residence of their mother, Julia Domna, and had Geta ambushed and murdered by centurions.  

Following this event, Caracalla carried out a purge that consumed the lives of more than 20,000 people. *(Even amid the slaughter, personal ties occasionally stayed the Praetorians’ hand: when soldiers sent to kill the brothers’ former tutor, Lucius Fabius Cilo, dragged him from his bath through the streets, Romans were in outcry until Caracalla intervened—“Insult not my father, strike not my tutor!”—and had the arresting officer put to death.)*  
   
But for now, it is still 211, and few are aware that terror looms.  
   
You will make three expeditions into Rome for each of the Spring, Summer, and Fall seasons of 211. The people you are likely to encounter on these expeditions are personages taken from this period in Roman history, many of whom will not survive Caracalla’s purges.  
   
You must navigate the many requests and demands of these patrons, keeping in mind the hostilities between the rival factions.

FACTIONS  
   
**Caracalla (Bassianus)** – RUBY – Born Lucius Septimius Bassianus and officially reigning as Marcus Aurelius Antoninus, the senior co-emperor would become known to history as **Caracalla** after the hooded Gallic cloak he popularized.  
***Caracalla enjoys the greatest favor with the Legiones out of any faction.***  
Caracalla is hostile to Geta and Plautilla, allied with Domna, and passively hostile to the Aurelians.  
Caracalla is mercurial and demanding.  
   
**Geta** – EMERALD – The junior co-emperor Geta will die at the end of this year, along with everyone associated with him in any way. Herodian describes that Geta was loved far more than Bassianus, while Cassius Dio describes them as duplicates of one another in all respects.  
Geta is hostile to Caracalla, and less so to Plautilla and the Aurelians, and is aligned with Domna.  
Geta is entitled and super cheap.  
   
**Domna** – DIAMOND – Julia Domna, empress mother to Bassianus (Caracalla) and Geta, is remembered for her patronage of the arts and for maintaining a learned circle of sophists and scholars. Domna loves both of her sons and wants nothing more than for them to stop fighting.  
Domna is hostile to Plautilla and the Aurelians, and allied with Caracalla and Geta.  
Domna is wealthy and generous.  
   
**Plautilla** – SAPPHIRE – Fulvia Plautilla was the daughter of Severus’ former second-in-command, Gaius Fulvius Plautianus. Plautilla was wed to Caracalla when they were both teenagers. However, Plautianus was executed after Caracalla alleged that Plautianus was plotting to murder Severus, and Plautilla was officially banished to the island of Lipara. Though exiled on paper, street rumor whispers that Plautilla has slipped back into Rome by night in disguise to conspire against the regime from the shadows—until Caracalla’s assassins catch up with her and her brother at year’s end.  
***Plautilla enjoys the most favor with the Nefarii.***  
Plautilla is most hostile to Caracalla, but also to Geta and Domna, and is allied with the Aurelians.  
Plautilla is desperate and poor.  
   
**Aurelius** – AMETHYST – Lucius Aurelius Commodus Pompeianus is the lead member of the Claudio-Aurelian family that ruled Rome for much of the preceding century. Pompeianus too was caught up in Caracalla’s purges, though Caracalla took the precaution of making Pompeianus’ murder appear to have been perpetrated by bandits.  
***Aurelius enjoys the most favor with the Patricii.***  
Hostile to Caracalla and Geta, less so to Domna, allied with Plautilla.  


**SET UP**  
   
1) Prepare the Phase 2 Delivery board by shuffling each of the **36** tile stacks (**A1** through **F6**) and placing each in its respective neighborhood cell of the grid.  
   
2) Prepare the Phase 1 Market board by shuffling the Goods Deck and dealing 24 Goods from the deck onto the Market board.  
   
When placing goods on the Market, place the good in the row of its respective category, in the rightmost available column of the row.  
(If a row already holds six goods, discard the Good in the rightmost column to the bottom of the Goods deck, push all Goods in the row to the right, and place the new Good in the leftmost column of the row – if this happens, there will be fewer than 24 Goods on the market at the start of the game).  
Once all 24 Goods have been placed on the Market, realign all of the Goods in each row so that the leftmost Good sits in the “Precious” (`<<`) column, and the other Goods in the row occupy the columns immediately to the right of the Good before it. (The “Abundant” (`>>`) column should only have a Good in it if there are six Goods in that category on the market).  
   
3) Shuffle the five **(5)** remaining decks: Patricii, Legiones, Nefarii, Missiones, and Influence.  
   
4) Distribute to each Player a matching set comprising a Cart Mat, a Cart, and two loan tokens.  
   
5) Distribute **12(d)** to each Player from the bank.  
   
6) Deal five **(5)** distinct Mission cards and five **(5)** distinct Influence cards to each Player.  
   
7) Players must choose two **(2)** Mission cards and two **(2)** Influence cards to discard to the bottom of their respective decks.  

8) **Political Climate Draft (6×3 Board)**: The **first Player to finish discarding** Influence and Mission cards claims **First Pick** on the **Political Climate & End-Game Modifiers** board (six columns: *Caracalla*, *Geta*, *Domna*, *Plautilla*, *Aurelians*, and *Missions*, with three options per column). **At most one (1) option may be enacted per column.**  
- **2 Players (4 Active Modifiers)**: Snake draft **`1st Discarder → Rival → Rival → 1st Discarder`** (the fastest discarder gets **first pick and last pick**!).  
- **3–6 Players (`min(N, 6)` Active Modifiers)**: Each Player enacts **1** modifier in the exact order they finished discarding.  

9) **Normal Game Turn Order & 5th/6th Player Stipend**: Once the Political Climate is set, give the **First Player Token** to the Player seated clockwise **after** the First Discarder—so the Player who claimed First Pick on the Political Climate acts **LAST** in normal turn order! In **5–6 Player games**, to offset late Spring Market position, the **5th Player** in Spring turn order receives **+1(d)** (**13(d)** total) and the **6th Player** receives **+2(d)** (**14(d)** total) from the bank.  
   
**Combat breaks all ties in the Game (including every Most/Least Political Climate modifier, even when tied at 0!). See COMBAT BETWEEN PLAYERS.**  
 

**PHASES**  
The game takes place over the course of three seasons (Spring, Summer, and Fall), each divided into three phases: The Market Phase, the Delivery Phase, and the Clean-Up Phase, followed by the Winter Reckoning at the end of Fall.  
   
MARKET PHASE  
The Market Phase takes place in the port of Ostia, represented by the Phase 1 Market board.  
   
In the Market Phase, players take turns buying goods from the Market that they will cart into Rome during Phase 2 to sell. *(Technically, under Caesar’s Lex Iulia Municipalis, wagons hauling marble for imperial public works were exempt from the daytime ban—a loophole Rome’s nocturnal teamsters and crooked clerks exploit shamelessly.)*  
   
Starting with the player with the First Player Token, and in this specific order:  
   
1) **Source**. Player *may* choose to discard an Influence card in order to rifle through the Goods deck, select a Good, and place it on the Market board in the leftmost column of its respective row, pushing all other Goods in the row to the right.

2) **Buy**. Player *may* then buy one Good from the Market board, paying the Buy Price for the Good listed in the box that corresponds to the column that the Good occupies.  
(i.e. A Good in the “Rare” column can be purchased for its `<` Buy Price).

3) **Replenish**. *If* the Player bought a Good from the Market, place the top card from the Goods Deck onto the Market board in the leftmost column of its respective category, pushing all other Goods in the row to the right (any Goods pushed off the Market are moved to the bottom of the Goods deck).

4) **Pass**. When a Player has completed their turn, play passes to the next Player in clockwise order.

5) **End Market Phase**. When a round passes with all players neglecting to buy a good from the Market, the Market Phase is over, and play proceeds to the Delivery Phase.  
**When the Market Phase ends, pass the First Player Token clockwise.**  
(If you are familiar with poker, this is the same; play proceeds once there has been a full round of play without buys.)  
(Discarding an Influence Card to source a Good onto the Market does not count as a Buy.)  
 

DELIVERY PHASE  
Players will spend up to 10 turns carting around Rome in the hope of completing missions and selling goods for a profit.  
   
Remember that this is a night delivery – you are racing against sunrise. Any Players still in Rome at the end of the 10th turn will pay a hefty fine!  
   
I) **Set the Clock**. Place the hourglass/turn clock token in the First Turn section of the clock board (8:00 P.M. – 9:00 P.M.).  
II) **Initial Scout**. Beginning with the Player with the First Player Token, each Player may look at the top tile of one of the tile stacks and put it back.  
III) **Start your engines.** Continuing in turn order, each Player selects a point of entry (any of the six neighborhoods that commences with “Via,” i.e.: **B1**, **E1**, **F3**, **F6**, **B6**, or **A4**). Entering Rome onto your chosen Via Gate from outside the walls counts as your Move on Turn 1 (8:00 P.M.), revealing that Gate tile and resolving its encounter on your first turn.  
   
Starting with the Player with the First Player Token:  
   
1) **Trash a tile or card** *(once per turn, before or after Moving — even while on a cell occupied by another merchant)*. Player *may* elect to discard an Influence Card in order to send either the top card of **any of the 6 decks** (**Patricii**, **Nefarii**, **Legiones**, **Missiones**, **Influence**—whose existing top card cycles *before* your discarded card goes underneath—or **Goods**), the top **unrevealed stack tile**, **or** an **exposed face-up neighborhood tile** to the bottom of its respective deck or tile stack. If the trashed exposed tile is **occupied** by any active merchant, the next unrevealed stack tile immediately flips face-up to replace it; if **unoccupied**, the cell remains **unrevealed** until a merchant enters it.

2) **Move**. Player *may* move once in any available direction (including exiting Rome off an open perimeter edge):  
Green: can pass through.  
Red: impassable.  
Angry Mob Icon: passable, but triggers Angry Mob table encounter.  
Theater Troupe Icon: passable, but triggers Theater Troupe table encounter.  
Soothsayer Icon: passable, but triggers Soothsayer table encounter.  
Legiones Icon: passable, but triggers Legiones Deck Encounter.  
Nefarii Icon: passable, but triggers Nefarii Deck Encounter.  
Patricii Icon: passable, but triggers Patricii Deck Encounter.  
2(d): Must pay **2 denarii** (or listed amount) in order to pass through this edge.  
*Complete Edge Encounters before moving to or revealing the next tile.*

**Crossing the Tiber River**: Heavy freight carts cannot ford the Tiber River diagonally between the West Bank (`A1`, `A4`, `B4`, `A5`) and the East Bank — all **diagonal crossings** (`NW`, `NE`, `SE`, `SW`) across the Tiber are completely blocked (even with *Shortcut*). Carts may cross between the West Bank and East Bank **orthogonally** (`N`, `S`, `E`, or `W`) along any passable tile edge (using the face-up tile's edge indicator as the source of truth), including across Rome’s five intra-urban Stone Bridges and adjacent Tiber crossings (paying the **+4(d) *Divided Tiber*** toll when enacted unless holding `≥ +1` Emerald or using *Shortcut*):
- **Pons Aelius**: **A1 ↔ B1** (*Mausoleum Hadriani* ↔ *Via Flaminia*)
- **Pons Neronianus**: **A1 ↔ A2** (*Mausoleum Hadriani* ↔ *Circus Agonalis*)
- **Pons Agrippae**: **A3 ↔ A4** (*Theatrum Pompeii* ↔ *Via Aurelia*)
- **Pons Fabricius**: **B4 ↔ C4** (*Trans Tiberim* ↔ *Mons Capitolinus*)
- **Pons Aemilius**: **B4 ↔ B5** (*Trans Tiberim* ↔ *Aventinus Major*)
- **Adjacent Orthogonal Crossings**: **B3 ↔ B4**, **A5 ↔ B5**, and **A5 ↔ A6** (crossable orthogonally when the tile edge allows)

3) **Reveal Tile**. Unless the cell being entered has a revealed tile with a **⤹** (persistent) icon, reveal the top unrevealed tile of the tile stack (moving the presently revealed tile to the bottom of the tile stack if appropriate). Then place Player’s Cart on the new cell.

4) **Complete Mission(s)**. Player *may* fulfill any missions that Player has that are relevant to the occupied cell.  
For Delivery Missions: Items can be picked up at this cell by moving the mission cargo onto the Player’s Cart mat. Items can be delivered at this cell immediately upon arrival (before any Step 5 Tile Encounter) by moving the mission card to the bottom of the Missiones Deck and claiming its rewards.  
For Sell Missions: Player may complete the mission during Step 7 (Transact) by selling **one (1)** matching Good—or any number of non-fenced **Grain** cards if it is a Grain Sell Mission—subject to the price modifier indicated on the Mission Card (e.g., if the mission is to “Sell Cheap Wine at `+` in C3”, and if the market scarcity for Wine is “Common/`>`”, then when Player arrives at C3, Player can sell 1 Cheap Wine for the amount listed in the “Uncommon/`=`” Sell Price column for that wine).

5) **Trigger Encounter**. If the revealed Tile has an encounter icon in the central field, trigger the appropriate Deck or Table encounter indicated by the icon(s).

6) **Refresh**. If the revealed Tile has a **⤿** icon, then move it to the bottom of the Tile stack after resolving the encounter, reveal the next Tile in the stack, and continue from Step 5.

7) **Transact**. Player may make one Sale, Purchase, or Gamble:  
- **Sell (1 Good per Hour — or Bulk Grain!)**: Player may sell **at most one (1) Good card** in a category printed in the white box on the revealed tile (or authorized by an active Sell Mission at this cell)—**EXCEPT for non-fenced Grain**, where a single hourly Transaction may sell **any number (or all!) of the Player’s Grain cards simultaneously** at the current Grain scarcity modifier. The Scarcity Modifier for the category affects the sell price for the Good(s) relative to present market scarcity. *(Fenced Encounter Mission cargo is always sold 1 card at a time.)*  
- **Purchase**: (On any of the three central Forum cells: **C3**, **D3**, **D4**) Player can purchase one good from the Market subject to the Scarcity Modifier listed on the tile.  
- **Gamble**: If the revealed tile has a Gambling/Dice icon, then the Player can elect to spend their transaction making a single Gamble of up to **20(d)**. See TABLE ENCOUNTERS – Gambling.

8) **Free Scout** – Once per Delivery turn—at any time **before or after Moving** and **before or after Transacting**—the Player may peek at the top unrevealed Tile of any cell stack or the top Card of any of the six Decks (**Patricii**, **Nefarii**, **Legiones**, **Goods**, **Missiones**, or **Influence**) and put the tile or card back on top. Scouting NEVER forces your turn to end; continue acting afterward or end your turn from the Scout preview.

9) **Advance Turn** – Once all Players have acted, advance the Clock one hour and pass the First Player Token clockwise. Repeat until Sunrise (the end of Turn 10) or until all Carts have left Rome.  
 

CLEAN-UP PHASE  
Clean-Up Phase is used to prepare for the following season.

1) **Pay Fines**. Any Players still in Rome pay a daybreak penalty. This penalty is halved if the Player is occupying a Gate (“Via”/entry point) Tile (**B1, E1, F3, F6, B6, A4**). See DAWN.

2) **Clean Up Rome**. Move all exposed tiles to the bottom of their respective stack.

3) **Buy Influence**. In turn order, Players can buy Influence cards with cash. The price of each card depends on the number of Influence Cards currently in Player’s hand (including any face-down Soothsayer cards):  
0–1 Cards: **2(d)**  
2–3 Cards: **4(d)**  
4 Cards: **6(d)**  
5+ Cards: **8(d)**  
    	   
4) **Take Out & Repay Loans.** At the end of Spring and Summer, Players have the option of taking out loans (accruing 50% compound interest per season):  
   
**Spring Loan**: Receive **20(d)** at the end of Spring.  
- Repay at end of Summer: **30(d)**  
- Repay at end of Fall / Deducted at Winter Reckoning: **45(d)**  
   
**Summer Loan**: Receive **40(d)** at the end of Summer.  
- Repay at end of Fall / Deducted at Winter Reckoning: **60(d)**  
   
5) **Pay Your Guards.** Players must pay any guards they wish to keep an amount equal to what their hiring cost would be if they were placed in the first exposed slot on the right of the market board (i.e. if there are three guards on the market, the Pay price is the Uncommon/`=` Hire price of the guard; if there are four guards on the market, the Pay price is the Common/`>` Hire price of the guard).

6) **Next Season**. Pass the First Player Token clockwise and advance to the next season (or proceed to Winter Reckoning after Fall). *(In the digital edition, during Fall Clean-Up Players may also toggle whether their Driver is authorized to expend Combat Influence cards during Winter Reckoning PvP tie-breakers.)*  
 

**ENCOUNTERS**  
Resolve an Encounter the moment Player occupies a tile with an Encounter icon in the center or passes through an Edge with an Encounter Icon on it.  
After resolving the Encounter, Player may complete movement and transactions.  
   
For **Deck Encounters**: Player draws the top card from the corresponding Deck Type.  
For **Table Encounters**: Player rolls a ® and consults the corresponding table.  
   
DECK ENCOUNTERS  
When a Deck Encounter is triggered, Player does the following:  
1) **Draw**. Reveal the top card of the corresponding deck.  
2) **Trigger Reaction**. Consult the highlighted bars in the middle of the card to see how the encountered character reacts to Player:  
i) **Identify Character’s Faction**. Indicated by the color of the highlighted bars and portrait outline, the faction gem icon, and the character bio.  
ii) **Calculate Player’s Favor (Uncapped)**. Count the number of positive Favor gems that Player has collected for the corresponding Faction and subtract the number of negated gems collected for that Faction (plus any temporary Favor modifier). **Faction Favor is uncapped** and can climb to `±5` or higher!  
iii) **Determine Character’s Reaction.** Compare Player’s Favor to the highlighted threshold bars on the encounter card.  
iv) **Apply Reaction**. Possible reactions include:  
a) **Combat Icon**: Initiate combat. *(Digital adaptation note: Players cannot voluntarily initiate combat against non-threatening encounters unless unlocked by a combat Mission or the ‘Casus Belli’ Influence card.)*  
b) **!! (Demand)**: Player must fulfill the demand or initiate combat. If Player is only partially able to fulfill the demand, Player can avoid combat by satisfying the demand to the greatest extent possible (taking Hostile Favor consequences). If Player has none of the thing being demanded (when noted on card), ignore the demand completely.  
c) **! (Request)**: Player must fulfill the request or take Hostile Favor consequences.  
d) **? (Offer)**: Player may partake in the offered transaction. This does not consume the Player’s transaction for the turn.  
e) **Quest / Mission**: Character demands or offers a Mission.  

DECK TYPES  
- **Patricii** – [Saffron] – *(A) Senators, aristocrats, and magistrates (30 cards: `Caracalla` 2, `Geta` 5, `Julia Domna` 8, `Plautilla` 3, `Aurelius` 8, `Events` 4).* Mechanics: Heavily aligned with Julia Domna & Aurelius; friendly nobles grant high-paying Missions and Denarii, while hostile nobles demand luxury wine/marble or bribes. Historically, patrician families controlled Rome's civic patronage and grain dole administration.
- **Legiones** – [Madder] – *(L) Praetorian guards, vigiles, and cohorts (30 cards: `Caracalla` 9, `Geta` 5, `Julia Domna` 3, `Plautilla` 8, `Aurelius` 3, `Events` 2).* Mechanics: Dominated by Caracalla and Plautilla; gains `+2 CP` in Combat whenever `Army Pay Hike` is active. Historically, Rome's Praetorians and night cohorts demanded protection bribes from merchants and were often as dangerous as street criminals.
- **Nefarii** – [Gray] – *(B) Bandits, cutthroats, and nocturnal syndicates (29 cards: `Caracalla` 5, `Geta` 5, `Julia Domna` 5, `Plautilla` 8, `Aurelius` 3, `Events` 3).* Mechanics: Plautilla's underworld network is densest here; `1×–3×` Dagger icons always draw **1 card** and multiply Combat to `X(R)+X` CP when fighting occurs. Historically, gangs, informants, and cutpurses ruled unlit alleys after curfew.

[Green] Can pass through.  
[Red] Cannot pass through.

STADIUM GAMBLING  
If the Gambling symbol is present on the revealed tile, Player can elect to spend their turn’s Transaction gambling:  
1) Player places a cash wager on top of the tile (**maximum 20(d) per gambling action**).  
2) Player rolls ® (or `2®` if *Match Fixing* is played).  
3) Player receives back cash equal to the total rolled times the bet:  
0: **Lose** – Wager goes to the bank.  
I: **Break even** – Wager is returned (`1×`).  
II: **Double Up** – Receive `2×` wager (`+1×` net profit).  
III: **Triple Up** – Receive `3×` wager (`+2×` net profit).  
*(The expected value of 1® is 1.167×; with Match Fixing’s 2®, up to a 6× payout is possible on the 20(d) maximum wager.)*  
   
**MISSIONS**  
A Player can have a **Maximum of five (5) active missions**. Whenever a Player takes on a **6th active mission** outside initial Setup (and it does not immediately auto-complete), the Player must immediately pick one of the 6 active missions to fail/cancel before taking further actions or ending their turn. **A Player can never end their turn with more than five (5) active missions.** There are three types of mission assignments:

1) **Starting Missions**. At the beginning of the game, Players receive five distinct missions and pick three to keep. These missions can be failed voluntarily (by canceling them at any time or when resolving a 6-mission overflow). There is no Favor penalty for failing Starting Missions *(unless **Bonded Couriers** is active, which fines **-10(d)** on every failed or unfinished mission)*, **and Goods picked up for Starting Mission delivery quests cannot be sold** (if abandoned, the cargo is discarded for nothing, never fenced).

2) **Deck Encounter Missions**. A character encountered in Rome may give the Player a mission. Goods picked up for Deck Encounter delivery missions *can* be fenced to an interested buyer for the Fenced Sell value on the card, which immediately fails the mission and triggers **Hostile Favor Consequences** from the patron *(unless **Smuggler’s Fence** is active, in which case fenced cargo sells **1 Scarcity step higher** and does **not** count as a Failed Mission, though Patron Hostile Favor still applies)*. If a Deck Encounter Mission is failed or canceled for any reason (including when resolving a 6-mission overflow or remaining unfinished at the end of Fall!), Player receives the **Hostile Favor Consequences** from the patron *(plus **-10(d)** if **Bonded Couriers** is active)*.  

3) **Soothsayer Missions**. Mandatory and cannot be failed voluntarily — a Soothsayer mission can **only** be canceled if **all six (6)** active missions are Soothsayer missions simultaneously *(in which case 1 Soothsayer mission is canceled with no penalty and its sealed Influence cards return to the Influence deck)*. Goods picked up for Soothsayer delivery missions cannot be sold, though they can be stolen.  

4) **Special Imperial Combat Missions (*Regicide* & *Regime Change*)**. Two high-stakes Encounter Missions carry an **“any Player”** clause:  
- **Regicide**: Completed if **any Player** defeats Emperor **Caracalla** in combat while you hold *Regicide*. Completing *Regicide* **immediately ends the game in an instant assassination victory** for the Player holding *Regicide*!  
- **Regime Change**: Completed if **any Player** defeats Co-Emperor **Geta** in combat while you hold *Regime Change*, awarding **100(d)** plus `+2 Rubies` and `+1 Diamond` while play continues normally.  

DEMANDS & COMBAT  
COMBAT  
1) Player takes (Hostile) Favor Consequences from the Encounter Card.  
2) Generate Player’s CP:  
- Player’s Driver has `1®` CP  
- `+` CP from Guards  
- `+` CP from Weapons *(Note: crated **Loricae** body armor carries **0 CP** in sudden street ambushes—too slow to strap on mid-attack, offset by its lucrative sell-to-buy margin!)*  
- `+` Mandatory Exotic Animals in Cart (must roll all Exotic Animals in combat, including *Ostrich* or any negative-die beasts!)  
- `+` `+1 CP` per positive Caracalla Ruby if **Army Pay Hike** is active  
3) Opponent rolls `(#)®` CP (unless card formula states otherwise; `+2 CP` to all `Legiones` NPCs if **Army Pay Hike** is active; *Distraction* deducts `1®` positive die from the target’s pool prior to rolling).  
4) All ® on both sides are rolled simultaneously so post-roll Influence cards (*Twist Of Fate*, *Flying Chamber Pot*, *Gladiator’s Intervention*) may modify the dice.  
5) If **Player’s CP ≥ Opponent’s CP**: **Player wins**, collects Combat Rewards (if any), and Player’s turn proceeds.  
Otherwise, **Opponent wins**:  
a) Player suffers `1` Wound (W) for each point of CP difference (after shield/helmet wound-ignore rolls). Guards absorb wounds first (Player chooses which Guard dies if holding multiple; dead Guards return to the bottom of the Market deck), and the Driver must survive if possible.  
b) Player’s turn ends.  
c) **If Player initiated combat by refusing a Demand, Player must still fulfill the refused Demand** (to the greatest extent possible from remaining cash/goods/influence).  
   
DEATH  
If the Driver dies, Player rolls ®:  
0: **Yep, dead.** Player loses all Goods, ends turn; Player’s night is over.  
I: **Left for dead.** Player loses all Goods, night continues.  
II: **Beaten and mugged.** Player loses all Cash, night continues.  
III: **Begged for mercy.** Player loses all Influence Cards, night continues.  
   
DAWN  
At the end of the 10th turn, any Player whose cart is still in the city pays a curfew fine:
- **Spring**: **12(d)** (`6(d)` on a Gate)  
- **Summer**: **20(d)** (`10(d)` on a Gate)  
- **Fall**: **32(d)** (`16(d)` on a Gate)  

If the entire fine cannot be covered in Cash, Player must pay the remainder in kind, forfeiting Goods with enough Uncommon (`=`) Sell Price value to satisfy the amount owed.

During Setup, Players draft active modifiers from the **6×3 Political Climate Board** (4 modifiers in a 2-Player game; `min(N, 6)` modifiers in 3–6 Player games; max 1 option per column):

After Fall Clean-Up concludes, December 211 arrives and each Player’s final score in **Denarii (d)** is settled in strict order:
1) **Unfinished Missions**: Any incomplete Missions fail automatically (counting as Failed Missions, fining **-10(d)** each if *Bonded Couriers* is active, and applying **Hostile Favor Consequences** for incomplete Deck Encounter Missions).
2) **Unsold Goods Liquidation**: Remaining non-mission Goods in a Player’s Cart liquidate at their **Abundant (`>>`) Sell Price**.
3) **Unpaid Loans**: Deduct **45(d)** for an unpaid Spring Loan and **60(d)** for an unpaid Summer Loan.
4) ** Political Climate Settlement**:
   - **Step 4a — Elimination FIRST** (*Imperial Proscription*, if active): The Player with **Least** Caracalla Favor is **eliminated immediately** before any other Political Climate awards or penalties resolve. Eliminated Players rank below all survivors, cannot win the game, and are **excluded from all subsequent Most/Least awards, penalties, steals, and percentage bonuses**!
   - **Step 4b — Flat Gem/Mission Reckoning & Penalties**: Among surviving Players, resolve enacted flat per-gem/per-mission modifiers and Least penalties (*Praetorian Backlash*, *Brother’s Grudge*, *Jealous Brothers*, *Court Neutrality*, *Smuggled Dowry*, *Old Senate Purse*, *Choose a Side*, *Master Purveyor*, *Guild Ledger*).
   - **Step 4c — Wealth Steal & Percentage Patronage**: Among surviving Players, resolve *Exile’s Informants* (`15(d)` stolen from the wealthiest surviving rival), followed by *Imperial Patronage* (`+20%` of positive wealth, rounded to nearest Denarius).

The surviving Merchant with the highest final Denarii total wins!

FAVOR SUMMARY  
- **Uncapped**: Faction Favor has **no cap** (`±5` or higher is possible).  
- **(Ruby) – Caracalla (Bassianus)**: Senior Emperor; loved by mother and troops; otherwise feared and despised.  
- **(Emerald) – Geta**: Junior Emperor; loved by mother and sycophants; murdered at year’s end.  
- **(Sapphire) – Plautilla**: Banished Empress conspiring in Rome by night; hated by Severans, loved by Rome’s underworld (*Nefarii*).  
- **(Amethyst) – Aurelius**: Old Senatorial Order; revered by aristocrats (*Patricii*), distrusted by the emperors.

COMBAT BETWEEN PLAYERS  
**1) Proactive PvP Duels via *Casus Belli* (Night Delivery Phase):**  
*"Initiate combat with a present opponent: exhausts transaction; cannot be used on Turn 1. Or, initiate combat in any Encounter."*  
- **Two Uses:** During an active **NPC Encounter** decision window (`pre_decision`), *Casus Belli* lets you initiate Combat against a friendly/unthreatening NPC (this encounter use is allowed on Turn 1 and does **not** spend your turn Transaction). Outside encounters, during the **Night Delivery Phase** (`Turn 2+` only—prohibited on **Turn 1**), you may play *Casus Belli* on your turn while sharing a neighborhood tile (`A1`–`F6`) with at least one rival merchant who has not yet Exited Rome, suffered Night Over, or been eliminated.  
- **Turn Cost & Timing:** Challenging a present rival **immediately exhausts your hourly Transaction `(T)`** for the turn (so you cannot challenge if you have already transacted, gambled, or ended your turn). If the Attacker loses combat (`Defender CP > Attacker CP`), the Attacker's current turn ends immediately; if the Attacker wins or ties, they may continue using any remaining Movement `(M)`.  
- **Simultaneous Combat & Consumables:** Both the Attacker and Defender decide whether to commit `"destroyed if used"` consumable Weapons before rolling. Both sides roll `1®` for their Driver + Guards + Weapons + mandatory Exotic Animals + Army Pay Hike (`+1 CP` per positive Ruby) + Pre-Roll Influence (*Mercenary Ambush* `+2®`, *Distraction* `-1®`), followed by simultaneous Post-Roll Influence (*Twist Of Fate*, *Gladiator’s Intervention* `+1®` CP, *Flying Chamber Pot* `+1 CP`). Unlike NPC combat (where ties favor the player), a **PvP Tie results in no Wounds and no Spoils**.  
- **Armor, Guards & Driver Fate Spoils:** If one merchant wins by `Winner CP > Loser CP`, the Loser suffers `Raw Wounds = Winner CP − Loser CP`. Armor (`ignoreWounds`) blocks Wounds first, then each remaining Wound kills `1` Guard (`▲`, hired Guards return to the bottom of the Market deck). If at least `1` unblocked Wound reaches the Loser's Driver, the Loser rolls `1®` on the **Driver Fate Chart** (and the Combat Winner claims all lost Spoils):  
  - **`0` — Dead:** **Combat Winner claims all Goods** on the Loser's Cart (Mission Cargo is discarded; non-mission Goods that exceed Winner Cart capacity go through overflow/swap and any excess returns to the bottom of the Market deck). The **Loser's night ends (`nightEnded`)** for the rest of the Season (no Dawn curfew fine is charged to that Cart).  
  - **`I` — Left for dead:** **Combat Winner claims all Goods** (with the same transfer/Mission Cargo discard rules), while the **Loser remains active** in Rome for future turns in the Season.  
  - **`II` — Beaten and mugged:** **Combat Winner steals all of the Loser’s Denarii `(d)`**; Loser's night continues.  
  - **`III` — Begged for mercy:** **Combat Winner claims all of the Loser’s Influence cards**; Loser's night continues.

**2) Winter Reckoning & Most/Least Tie-Breaker Combat:**  
**All ties in the game**—including every **Most** or **Least** Political Climate award (even when Players are tied at `0`!), tied wealthiest rivals, and Final Score ties at Winter Reckoning—are broken by **Combat between the tied Players**, using Drivers (`1®`), Guards, Weapons, mandatory Exotic Animals, combat Influence cards, and `+1 CP` per positive Ruby if *Army Pay Hike* is active.  
- **Pre-Roll vs. Post-Roll Combat Influence**: Pre-roll cards (*Mercenary Ambush* `+2®`, *Distraction* deducting `1®` positive die from the opponent’s pool prior to rolling) apply before dice are cast; all ® on both sides roll simultaneously; and reactive post-roll cards (*Twist Of Fate* rerolling a low positive die, a high enemy die, or a negative Exotic Animal die; *Flying Chamber Pot* granting `+1 CP` after dice are rolled; *Gladiator’s Intervention* rolling `+1®` bonus CP after combat dice are rolled) may be played when they can overturn a deficit or tie. *(In the digital edition, Players may opt out of auto-spending Combat Influence cards during Winter Reckoning tie-breakers during Fall Clean-Up.)*  
- **Strictly One Recipient**: Every Most/Least bonus or penalty is awarded to **strictly one Player**. For **Most** awards, the Combat **winner** claims the bonus; for **Least** penalties or elimination, the Combat winner escapes and the Combat **loser** suffers the penalty!  
- **No Draws**: If a Combat between Players ends in a draw, repeat Combat (any one-shot Influence cards or consumable Weapons used in a previous round remain exhausted) until one side wins by at least **1 CP**!

## SYMBOL & ICON REFERENCE

### Goods & Guards Card Symbols
- **Bold Green `+𐆖` (Sell / Fence Price Row)**: The top row on a Good Card’s price table. Indicates the **Denarii (`𐆖`) you receive** when selling that Good in Rome (or Fencing Encounter Mission Cargo) at each Scarcity column (`<<` to `>>`).
- **Italic Red `-𐆖` (Buy / Hire Price Row)**: The bottom row on a Good or Guard Card’s price table. Indicates the **Denarii (`𐆖`) you pay** to purchase that Good at Portus Ostiae (or an Imperial Forum) or to hire/retain a Guard.
- **Denarius (`𐆖` / `(d)`)**: Roman silver coin (`U+10196`) used for all purchases, sales, road tolls, Stadium wagers, Guard upkeep, and final victory scoring.
- **`<<`  `<`  `=`  `>`  `>>` (5 Scarcity Columns)**:
  - `<<` **Precious** (`0` cards remaining on that category’s Ostia Market track)
  - `<` **Rare** (`1` card remaining)
  - `=` **Uncommon** (`2–3` cards remaining)
  - `>` **Common** (`4` cards remaining)
  - `>>` **Abundant** (`5–6` cards remaining)
- **`■` (Cargo Space Cubes)**: Brown squares indicating how many of your Cart Mat’s **6 Cargo blocks (`6■`)** a Good occupies (`1■`, `2■`, or `3■`).
- **`▲` (Guard Seat Triangles)**: Gold triangles indicating how many of your Cart Mat’s **2 Guard seats (`2▲`)** a hired Guard occupies (`1▲` or `2▲`).

### Rome Tile, Action, & Encounter Icons
- **`(M)` (Blue Move Circle)**: Represents **1 Cart Movement** step along an open street edge.
- **`(T)` (Gold Transaction Circle)**: Represents **1 hourly Transaction** (Sell 1 Good, Bulk-Sell all Grain, Buy 1 Forum Good, or Gamble at a Stadium).
- **`(R)` / `®` (Roman Numeral Die)**: 6-sided custom Roman die with faces **`0, 0, I, I, II, III`** (average roll = `1.0`).
- **`++`  `+`  `=`  `-`  `--` (Tile Buyer & Forum Modifiers)**: Shifts the active Market Scarcity column 1 or 2 steps left toward `<<` Precious (`+` / `++`) or right toward `>>` Abundant (`-` / `--`).
- **Persistent (`⤹`) & Refresh (`↻` / `⤿`) Tile Icons**: Persistent landmark tiles remain face-up across seasons; Refresh tiles discard after their encounter resolves and immediately reveal the next tile.
- **Green Bar** (*Tile Edge*): Open Street — passable movement between connected neighborhoods without a Shortcut card.
- **Red Bar** (*Tile Edge*): Barricaded Alley — impassable barrier unless crossing with a `Shortcut` Influence card.
- **Encounter Icons**:
  - **Patricii** (*Senatorial Bust*): Always draws **1** card from the Patricii deck (30 cards: `Julia Domna` 8, `Aurelius` 8, `Geta` 5, `Plautilla` 3, `Caracalla` 2, Events 4); if `1×–3×` Combat occurs, roll `X(R)+X` CP where `X` = icon count.
  - **Legiones** (*Roman Helmet*): Always draws **1** card from the Legiones deck (30 cards: `Caracalla` 9, `Plautilla` 8, `Geta` 5, `Julia Domna` 3, `Aurelius` 3, Events 2; `+2 CP` under Army Pay Hike). Historically, Praetorians and soldiers extorted merchants and were often as dangerous as street criminals. If Combat occurs, `X` = icon count (`X(R)+X`).
  - **Nefarii** (*Dagger / `2×` / `3×` Daggers*): Always draws **1** card from the Nefarii underworld deck (29 cards: `Plautilla` 8, `Caracalla` 5, `Geta` 5, `Julia Domna` 5, `Aurelius` 3, Events 3). The number of daggers (`1`, `2`, or `3`) sets **`X`** in **`X(R)+X` Combat** (for example, `3× Daggers` rolls **`3(R)+3` CP**, NOT 3 cards).
  - **Angry Mob** (*Torch & Pitchfork*): Roll `1(R)` (`0`: lose Wine; `I`: lose Grain; `II`: lose costliest Good; `III`: safe).
  - **Theater Troupe** (*Comedy/Tragedy Masks*): Roll `1(R)` (`0`: lose positive Favor; `I`: lose negative Favor; `II`: lose rest of turn & next turn; `III`: draw `1(R)` Influence).
  - **Soothsayer** (*Augur’s Hand*): Draws a mandatory Soothsayer Mission and seals `1(R)` face-down Influence cards beneath it until fulfilled.
  - **Stadium Dice**: Spend your hourly Transaction to wager `1(d)–20(d)` and roll `1(R)` to win `Wager × Roll`.
